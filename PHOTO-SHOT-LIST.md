## How to shoot them

**Time of day.** Early morning or late afternoon. Midday sun in the Eastern
Province blows out the highlights and puts hard black shadows under everything.
Overcast is ideal if you ever get it.

**Angle.** Three-quarter front — stand off one front corner so you see the front
and one side at once. Machines look longer and more substantial this way than
straight-on. Crouch slightly below the cab line; it makes equipment look bigger.

**Frame.** Machine fills roughly 70–80% of the width. Landscape orientation, not
portrait — the cards are 3:2 wide.

**Background.** Walk around the machine until the background is as plain as you
can get: open yard, sky, a wall. Avoid other machines overlapping, parked cars,
scattered pallets, people mid-task.

**Clean-ish, not showroom.** Site dust is fine and honest. Rust, damage, flat
tyres and open panels are not — those read as a badly maintained fleet.

**Consistency matters more than perfection.** Nine photos taken the same morning,
same angle, same light will look like a professional set even from a phone.
Nine great photos taken in different light will look like a scrapbook.

## Also worth capturing while you're there

- **A wide yard shot** — several machines together. Strong candidate to replace
  the stock hero on the home page.
- **Crew working** — a real operator or welder in PPE. Would replace the stock
  welder on the manpower section.
- **One machine per owned unit** for the fleet detail pages later.

Faces: either get the person's agreement to appear on the website, or shoot from
behind / helmet-on so nobody is identifiable.
